# Architecture — Neural Surrogate vs. POD-Galerkin ROM for Burgers' Equation

Status: planning document. Every section is a plan, not a report of completed work.

## 1. Research problem

**Central question:** for the 1D viscous Burgers' equation, does a data-driven neural surrogate
(trained on an ensemble of solver-generated trajectories across varying initial conditions/
viscosity) predict new solution instances more accurately, and at what runtime speedup, than a
classical linear POD-Galerkin reduced-order model — and how does each method's accuracy degrade
when evaluated on parameters outside the training range (extrapolation) versus within it
(interpolation)?

**Motivation:** surrogate/reduced-order modelling is the named "reduced-order models, ML-enhanced
simulation" requirement in the Quaini (TUM) posting, and more broadly one of the most reused
capabilities across scientific-ML, computational-engineering, and digital-twin applications.
Burgers' equation is the standard nonlinear benchmark in this literature (used by, e.g., the
Fourier Neural Operator and DeepONet papers), giving established baselines and known failure modes
to validate against.

**Novelty classification:** comparative study / new application of established methods — not a
methodological contribution. Do not describe this as novel without new evidence to support that.

## 2. Mathematical formulation

**Governing equation:**
$$u_t + u\,u_x = \nu\, u_{xx}, \qquad x \in [0, L],\ t \in [0, T]$$
with initial condition $u(x, 0) = u_0(x; \boldsymbol\theta)$ parameterised by $\boldsymbol\theta$
(e.g., amplitude and location of an initial pulse) and periodic boundary conditions. Nonlinearity
in the advection term produces steep gradients for small $\nu$ — the interesting regime for
surrogate accuracy.

**Classical ROM (POD-Galerkin):** compute a low-rank basis $\{\phi_i\}_{i=1}^r$ via SVD of an
ensemble of solver-generated snapshots, project the PDE onto that basis, and integrate the reduced
system — a linear-subspace reduced model.

**Neural surrogate:** a network $g_\phi(\boldsymbol\theta) \mapsto u(x, t)$ trained by supervised
regression on (parameter, solver-generated trajectory) pairs — a nonlinear, data-driven ROM.

**Evaluation formulation:** for held-out parameters $\boldsymbol\theta$ split into
in-distribution (interpolation) and out-of-distribution (extrapolation) sets, compare both methods
against the ground-truth solver on accuracy (relative $L^2$ error) and wall-clock runtime.

## 5. Methods

- **Ground-truth solver:** finite-difference or pseudo-spectral, 1D, small grid — planned, not
  implemented.
- **Classical baseline:** POD-Galerkin ROM (SVD-based basis, projected dynamics).
- **Learned model:** CNN or MLP surrogate mapping parameters to the full space-time solution field
  — MVP scope. **Not planned for MVP:** a full Fourier Neural Operator — named explicitly as a
  research extension once the MVP comparison exists, to avoid overengineering the first version.

## 7. Evaluation

- **Metrics:** relative $L^2$ error against the ground-truth solver; wall-clock runtime per
  evaluation.
- **Baselines:** POD-Galerkin ROM is required, not optional — the neural surrogate must be shown to
  beat a real classical alternative, not nothing.
- **Robustness/failure analysis:** explicit inspection of both methods' behaviour near steep
  gradients (small $\nu$) — the known hard regime for both linear ROMs and neural surrogates on
  Burgers'-type equations.
- **Reproducibility:** fixed seeds, documented solver/network hyperparameters, pinned dependency
  versions once a working environment exists.

## 10. Limitations and risks

- **Scope risk:** POD-Galerkin ROM implementation is itself nontrivial (SVD basis + Galerkin
  projection is more than a one-line call); budget real time for it, don't treat it as free.
- **Solver risk:** the ground-truth solver must handle Burgers' steep-gradient regime without
  spurious oscillation (a known numerical-methods issue) — may require a modest upwind/flux-limiting
  scheme rather than naive central differences; this is a real implementation decision to make
  during, not before, coding.
- **Compute:** laptop-scale throughout; no cluster/HPC access assumed, consistent with the honest
  disclosure already present elsewhere in this portfolio.
